# McGlynn / Carroll line: research log

Researched 17 November 2026 on Ancestry, Find a Grave and Newspapers.com thumbnails. FamilySearch was signed out. This builds on the main session's drafts for McGlynn-104 (John, 1893–1942), McGlynn-468 (Joseph, b. 1855) and Ellen Carroll (b. 1865). Those drafts already cover the 1900, 1915, 1930 and 1940 censuses, the 1887 New York State marriage index, the WWI draft card and the Bayonne Times obituaries.

## What was already known
- **Joseph McGlynn**, b. Mar 1855, New York (1900 census). Married **Ellen Carroll** on 28 Apr 1887 at Sing Sing (Ossining), NY (NYS marriage index, certificate 5841). In Bayonne Ward 2 by 1900.
- **Ellen (Helen) Carroll**, b. Jun 1865, Ireland (County Cork per family). Died Dec 1932, Bayonne. Her obituary (Bayonne Times, 22 Dec 1932) names her brothers **Michael and Patrick**.
- **Children:**
  - Joseph Jr. (b. Feb 1888, NY)
  - John (b. 9 Jan 1893, NY; "Albany" per his obituary)
  - Mary (b. May 1896, NJ; later Mrs. Arthur Webber)
  - Anna (Mrs. William MacGillvray)
  - Thomas, named in John's 1942 obituary

## New findings

### 1. Ellen was a widow by 1930 and gave an immigration year of 1902
"1930 United States Federal Census", database with images, Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/6224/records/18424011 : accessed 17 November 2026), Helen McGlynn in household of William Macgillvery, 65 West 14th Street, Bayonne, Hudson, New Jersey; dwelling 44, family 107.
- Helen McGlynn, mother-in-law, 62, **widowed**, born Ireland (indexed "Northern Ireland"), both parents born Ireland. Immigration year **1902**, naturalized.
- The rest of the household: William Macgillvery 27, head; Anna 27, wife; and their daughters Helen 5 and Doris 4.
- **What it shows:**
  - Joseph died before April 1930. Since Helen headed the household in the 1915 NJ census, he probably died before 1915. The "spouse Joseph" in her 1932 obituary index refers to her late husband.
  - Daughter Anna was born about 1903. She was not yet born in 1900, when Ellen had 3 living children.
  - The immigration year 1902 contradicts her 1887 New York marriage. It may record a later trip to Ireland and back, which would mean a 1902 arrival list exists.
- **"Northern Ireland"** is the indexer's standardization; the image was not checked. Family tradition says County Cork.

### 2. Ellen's obituary clipping exists but couldn't be read
"U.S., Newspapers.com Obituary Index, 1800s-current", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/61843/records/1678018092 : accessed 17 November 2026). Clipping on Newspapers.com page image 909338190.
- The thumbnail headline reads roughly "Mrs. McGlynn, 2? Years Here, Dies". The number is unreadable, and it may give her years in Bayonne.
- The full clipping needs a Newspapers.com subscription (or the Bayonne library's microfilm). It should give her brothers' surnames and towns, her church, her cemetery and possibly her Irish county.

## Searched with no useful result
- **Joseph, 1860/1870/1880 censuses** (Ancestry, soundex McGlynn, b. 1853–1857 NY): no identifiable match.
  - 1870 has a Joseph Glynn, 15, in Manhattan Ward 10 with Margaret (70), May (17) and Margaret (12). It also has a Joseph McGlinn, 13, alone in Manhattan Ward 18 (2nd enumeration).
  - 1860 has a Jos McGloin, b. about 1858, Manhattan Ward 6.
  - None is linked to our Joseph.
- **Joseph's death:** Ancestry's NJ indexes have no Bayonne Joseph McGlynn for 1890–1930. The Joseph McGlynn who died in Newark on 6 Oct 1890 was born about 1858; he is a different man, since ours was alive in 1900. Find a Grave has no matching Joseph or Ellen.
- **1910 census:** the Bayonne McGlynn household isn't indexed under McGlynn, Glynn or similar spellings.
- **1920 census:** Helen/Ellen McGlynn wasn't found.
- **NY State birth index** (for Joseph Jr. 1888 and John 1893, which would give the town): it isn't on Ancestry. It is free on FamilySearch and on the Internet Archive (Reclaim the Records scans).
- **Ellen Carroll in 1880 (Westchester/Albany):** no match. She probably arrived after 1880.

## What would break this open
1. **The 1887 marriage certificate** (NYS no. 5841). NY State marriage certificates of this period list both sets of parents, so it would give the parents of both Joseph and Ellen. Order it from the NYS Department of Health (genealogy copy). Or ask the Ossining town clerk, who holds a local copy and is often cheaper and faster.
2. **The Catholic marriage register**, St. Augustine's Church, Ossining, April 1887. It gives the parents and often Ellen's Irish parish. Archdiocese of New York registers are indexed on Findmypast. Or write to the parish.
3. **Ellen's NJ death certificate**, Bayonne, Dec 1932. It gives her parents' names and the informant. Order it from the NJ Department of Health; the FamilySearch "New Jersey Deaths" index may also cover it.
4. **Joseph's death** between 1900 and 1915, probably in Bayonne: the NJ State Archives death index (1901–1915) and FamilySearch.
5. **1905 NJ state census** (FamilySearch): the whole family in Bayonne, including Joseph if he was still alive.
6. **NY State birth index, 1888 and 1893** (FamilySearch or archive.org): the town where Joseph Jr. and John were born, possibly Albany. That would lead to Joseph's own family in Albany.
7. **Ellen's 1902 arrival** (if the 1930 census is right): a passenger list would name her Irish home town or nearest relative.
8. **Irish civil birth registration** (irishgenealogy.ie, free) for Ellen Carroll, Jun 1865, Co. Cork. It is only usable once her parents' names are known from #1–3, because Carroll is too common to pick her out otherwise.
