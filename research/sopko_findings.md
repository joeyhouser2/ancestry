# Sopko / Macko line: research log

Researched 18 November 2026 on Ancestry and FamilySearch (signed in). This builds on the main session's drafts `NEW-Sopko-John`, `NEW-Sopko-Mary` and `Sopko-5`. Those already cover the 1900 and 1910 censuses: John Sopko, b. Jul 1866, Hungary/Austria, arrived 1885–86, married about 1892; Mary, b. Oct 1875; children; Macko boarders.

**Goal:** find the home village in "Hungary", which is probably today's eastern Slovakia, and then the parish registers.

## New findings

### 1. Mary Sopko's maiden name was Macko, and she died in 1947, not 1942
"U.S., Newspapers.com Obituary Index, 1800s-current", database, Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/61843/records/1200731706 : accessed 18 November 2026), Mary Sopko, maiden name **Macko**, Bayonne; obituary in *The Bayonne Times*, **5 Feb 1947**. Spouse John. Children Mary McGlynn, Anna Osbahr, Veronica Peregrin, Agnes, Elisabeth, John Sopko and Steven Sopko.
- **What it proves:** the maiden name Macko is now backed by a record, not just family recollection. The list of children ties this Mary to Sopko-5 (Mary McGlynn). She died about early February 1947; the family remembered 1942, aged 75. Her age at death isn't indexed; the 1900 census gives a birth in Oct 1875.
- Related index entries, also from The Bayonne Times: Steven Sopko, mother Mary, 5 Feb 1947 (1164358439); "Mine Elizabeth Sopko", 5 Feb 1947 (1109688961); Mary Sopko, 7 Feb 1947 (1201056955). These are probably the same obituary and a funeral notice, indexed under different family members.

### 2. John Sopko died about September 1923
"U.S., Newspapers.com Obituary Index, 1800s-current", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/61843/records/2120492848 : accessed 18 November 2026), John Sopko, Bayonne; obituary in *The Bayonne Times*, **21 Sep 1923**.
- The index lists his wife Mary and children Elizabeth, Veronica, Agnes, John, Stephen, Andrew, Mary McGlynn and Anna Osbahr. The automatic indexing scrambles some of them into "siblings".
- **What it proves:** John died in 1923, before his wife. This fits the photo captioned "John Sopko ~1923".

### 3. Boarders in 1900 and 1910: probably relatives or fellow villagers
- **1910**, 34 John St, Bayonne Ward 5 (Ancestry 1910 census 7884/16332405, 16332395, 195515094). All three were laborers at the oil works and spoke Slovak:
  - **Andrew Macko**, 32, b. about 1878, Hungary, arrived 1901, single
  - **George Macko**, 27, b. about 1883, Hungary, arrived 1903, single
  - **Stephen Macko**, 22
- **1900** (FamilySearch, ark 1:1:M9JN-V6S): boarders **Joseph Dzvonar** (probably Zvonár) and **John Hrobfko**(?).
- **What it suggests:** the Mackos are probably Mary's brothers or cousins. Their Ellis Island lists of 1901 and 1903 should give their last residence (the village) and the relative they were joining.
  - Ancestry's index didn't surface them under Macko with the right birth year. Lists of that time use Hungarian or Slovak given names (András/Ondrej, György/Juraj) and often misspell the surname (Maczko, Matzko).
  - They may also have landed at Philadelphia or Baltimore.

### 4. A John Sopko naturalized in Passaic in 1902: probably NOT ours
"New Jersey, County Naturalization Records, 1749-1986", FamilySearch (https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:6Z3T-TNMM : accessed 18 November 2026), John Sopko, 37, born "Scwidnic, Austria" (probably **Svidník**, Slovakia), naturalized 15 Sep 1902, Passaic Common Pleas.
- On the image, this man lived at 16(?) First St, **City of Passaic**. He was a watchman and arrived at New York in April(?) 1888. His witness was John Servas, a saloonkeeper of 75 Third St, Passaic.
- **Assessment:** our John was in Bayonne from 1893 to 1923 and gave 1885–86 as his arrival year, so this is probably a different John Sopko. Svidník is still worth keeping in mind: Sopko is a common surname in the Svidník–Stropkov area of northeastern Slovakia.

### 5. CONFIRMED (19 Nov 2026): Stefan Maczko was going to his brother-in-law John Sopko in Bayonne
"New York, U.S., Arriving Passenger and Crew Lists (including Castle Garden and Ellis Island), 1820-1957", database with images, Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/7488/records/4031158163 : accessed 19 November 2026), Stefan Maczko, 16, arrived New York 19 Apr 1904 on the *Zeeland* from Antwerp; NARA T715 roll 448, image NYT715_448-0166 (Ancestry frame 41 of 54), line 16.
- As read from the image: single, laborer, cannot read or write; nationality Hungary; race **Slovak**; last residence **"Kószek"**; final destination **"Bayonne City"**; ticket paid by brother-in-law; going to join **"brother in law John Sopko, [2 or 3]4 John St, Bayonne"**.
- **What it proves:** the Macko boarders are Mary Sopko's **brothers** (John Sopko is Stefan's brother-in-law), and the family's village was written "Kószek". The Sopkos lived at 34 John St in 1910; the house number here is hard to read.

### 6. György Maczko (1903) was going to a brother in Bayonne, from the same village
Same collection (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/7488/records/4001805513 : accessed 19 November 2026), Gyorgy Maczko, 18, arrived 27 Feb 1903 on the *Königin Luise* from Bremen; NARA T715 roll 325, image NYT715_325-0700 (Ancestry frame 12 of 140; Foundation reference T715-0325-325), line 12. Foundation passenger ID JFBS-W5S.
- As read from the image: single, laborer, Hungary, race Slovak; last residence **"Kőszeg"**-like (indexed "Roszeg" by Ancestry, "Koskey" by the Foundation); destination **Bayonne, N.J.**; joining a **brother**, whose name looks like "Maczko András", Bayonne, N.J. (hard to read).
- **What it shows:** George Macko of the 1910 census, and through him Andrew Macko (already in Bayonne by 1903, matching the 1910 census's arrival year of 1901). So the three Macko boarders are brothers of Mary Sopko.

### 7. The village: probably Kysak (Hungarian Sároskőszeg), Sáros County, near Košice
- "Kószek/Kőszeg" is the Hungarian name element *kőszeg*. The famous Kőszeg is a town in western Hungary (Vas County), which had no Slovak population to speak of, so it doesn't fit two Slovak laborers.
- **Kysak**, a Slovak village in today's Košice-okolie District, was called **Sároskőszeg** in Hungarian (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kysak). Emigrants commonly dropped the county prefix ("Sáros-") on the lists.
- **Assessment:** Kysak is the likely village, but it is an identification from the place name, not yet proven. Confirm it in the Kysak-area Roman Catholic (or Greek Catholic) registers: Mary Macko's baptism about Oct 1875, György about 1885, Stefan about 1888, Andrew about 1875–78; and the Sopko–Macko marriage if it was there.

## Searched with no useful result
- **FamilySearch death indexes:** John Sopko (1923) and Mary Sopko (1947) are not indexed. FamilySearch's NJ births index has only index entries for the children Veronika (9 Jun 1907), Stephan (8 Dec 1909) and John (1 Jan 1911), with no parents' details.
- **FamilySearch naturalization index:** our John (naturalized by 1910 per the census) isn't indexed. His papers would be in Hudson County court records.
- **Archdiocese of Newark cemeteries:** no Sopko burials. As Rusyn or Slovak Greek Catholics, the family was probably buried in a parish cemetery.

## What would confirm the village (Kysak/Sároskőszeg is the working answer, see §5–7)
1. **Kysak church registers** on FamilySearch ("Slovakia Church and Synagogue Books, 1592-1935"): find the parish that served Kysak, then Mary Macko's baptism (Oct 1875) and her brothers'. Parents' names there would extend the Macko line.
2. **Mary Sopko's NJ death certificate, Feb 1947**, from the NJ Department of Health. It gives her parents and birthplace, though usually only the country.
3. **The full 1947 and 1923 obituaries** (Bayonne Times, Newspapers.com or Bayonne library microfilm). They name the funeral church and burial ground, and sometimes "a native of ___".
4. **The church registers.** Bayonne's Greek Catholic/Rusyn parish (St. John the Baptist) or Slovak parish holds the baptisms of the children from 1893 to 1912. Their registers often record the parents' native village, and the Sopkos' marriage about 1892 may be there too.
5. **John's naturalization** (Hudson County Court of Common Pleas, about 1890–1910). Hudson County petitions may give a birthplace.
6. **Mary Jr. born 7 Jul 1895 "in Hungary"** (family story; the 1900 census says NJ). If true, mother and baby returned about 1895–96, and their arrival list should name the village.
7. **Once a village is found:** "Slovakia Church and Synagogue Books, 1592-1935" (free on FamilySearch, often indexed) has the Greek Catholic and Roman Catholic registers. Look for John Sopko (b. Jul 1866), Mary Macko (b. Oct 1875) and their marriage, if it was in Europe.
