# Currier and Cilley lines: verification and arrival in America

Researched 19 November 2026. I checked each generation against the published town vital records and the standard compiled genealogies, all read in full text on archive.org. I also checked Ancestry for the 1878 death record. Nothing was downloaded to the repo.

Line: Lillian J. Currier (1890) ← Lawson W. Currier ← **Benjamin Currier (1826–1892)** ← **Ebenezer Currier (1793–1878) & Betsey Cilley (1795–1863)**. From that couple:
- **Currier line**, through Ebenezer, back to the immigrant **Richard Currier**.
- **Cilley line**, through Betsey, back to the **Sealy** fishermen of the Isles of Shoals.

## Short answer
- **Curriers: in Salisbury, Massachusetts, by 1641.**
  - **Richard Currier** received land in Salisbury in 1641 and 1642.
  - A 1664 court deposition puts his birth at about 1617. In it, he says he came as a **servant of Mr Francis Dove**, a landowner "of Salisbury in Old England" (Wiltshire).
  - No passenger list names him.
  - **Update (19 Nov, later):** I read the St Thomas, Salisbury register. It has "Richard the sonn of Thomas Curryer" christened **6 March 1616/17**, not 3 May 1616 as WikiTree says. That date fits both of his depositions (about 47 in 1664, about 66 in 1682). His master was of the same city. This makes the identification **probable**, though no record states it outright. See "Follow-up on the leads" below.
  - The family stayed in Salisbury/Amesbury for four generations. They moved to New Hampshire (Canterbury, Gilmanton, Andover) after about 1760.
- **Cilleys: at the Isles of Shoals (off Portsmouth, NH) by about 1647–1653.**
  - They were the **Sealy** brothers from **Stokeinteignhead, Devon**, fishermen and mariners.
  - The name later became Cilley.
  - Every authority agrees on that family. They disagree on **which brother or son was Benoni Cilley's father**, so the exact immigrant ancestor is unproven:
    - Richard Sealy, magistrate at the Shoals in 1653 (Cilley pamphlet, 1877)
    - an unnamed son of Richard (Hoyt, 1897–1919)
    - Thomas Sealy, baptised 1638 in Devon and in Maine in the 1660s (W. G. Davis, 1931/1956, "probable"). This is what WikiTree follows.
  - Benoni was in Salisbury/Seabrook by 1703.
- **Two links rest on compiled genealogies rather than original records.** They are flagged below:
  - Ebenezer (1793) → Ebenezer (1756)
  - Benjamin Cilley (about 1713) → Benoni

**WikiTree problems on the Currier side (for a later edit, not changed):**
- **Currier-44's death.** WikiTree gives 1796 at Amesbury. The 1910 Currier genealogy says 1814 at Canterbury, NH.
- **Currier-32 (Thomas, born 1590) and Currier-249 (Thomas, born 1553, "Glasgow").** Currier-32's own biography doubts the parentage. Hoyt and the 1910 genealogy give Richard no proven parents. The Glasgow birth has no source.
- **Ebenezer and Betsey's marriage date.** The Andover history gives both 1818 and 1820. The state marriage card (image read) settles it as **4 Jul 1820**; see Follow-up.
- **Currier-31's baptism date.** WikiTree gives 3 May 1616. The register reads **6 Mar 1616/17** (image read; see Follow-up).

**When the Cilleys came:** the Sealy brothers of Stokeinteignhead were at the Isles of Shoals from about 1647 (John, commander of the ship *Dolphin*). Richard Sealy was a Shoals magistrate in 1653. If WikiTree's Thomas is right, he was in Maine (the Kennebec) in the 1660s and took the oath of allegiance in 1679 [Cilley pamphlet; WikiTree Sealey-123 citing Davis]. Their father Andrew Sealy's 1664 will left legacies to sons "of the Isle of Shoals in New England" (WikiTree Sealie-17 citing Davis; not seen).

## Follow-up on the leads (19 Nov 2026, later)
1. **Ebenezer and Betsey's marriage: settled as 4 Jul 1820 (image read).**
   - Record: "New Hampshire, U.S., Marriage and Divorce Records, 1659-1947", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/5241/records/347554 : accessed 19 Nov 2026), image 2111 of 4973.
   - The card reads: groom Ebenezer Currier, bride Betsey Cilley, married by William Proctor, Justice of the Peace, July 4, 1820.
   - The index ("Marriage Records Index, 1637-1947", 2554/47713) gives the clerk's town as **New London**.
   - So the Andover history's "1818" is wrong. Their eldest son George, born "April, 1818" in that history, was either born before the marriage or has the wrong year.
2. **The pensioner's age: fits the 1756 birth (image read).**
   - The 1820 property schedule in pension file W. 724 (images 57–58) begins: "July 11 1820, Ebenezer Currier 2d aged 64 years resident in Andover". That puts his birth about 1756.
   - The household was "a wife 54 years totally unable to labor" (Mercy), plus "2 Children aged 16 & 13".
   - **So the Andover pensioner is the Ebenezer born 28 Mar 1756 at Amesbury. This is now strong.**
   - The schedule does not name his grown son. Ebenezer (born 1793), who was 27 and soon to marry, was no longer in the household.
   - The link Ebenezer (1793) → Ebenezer (1756) still rests on these points:
     - the Andover history ("son of Ebenezer Currier")
     - Ebenezer Sr. and Jr. both in Andover (school district list; the 1810 and 1820 censuses)
     - the pensioner's first wife Lydia Bean, whose children were born 1783–1790
   - **Status: probable–strong.**
3. **Caution: a second Ebenezer Currier was born about 1793.**
   - He lived in **Gilmanton/Belmont** in the 1850–1880 censuses and died about 1883 (tree data gives a birth of 24 Mar 1793).
   - Our Ebenezer was born 3 Sep 1793 in Gilmanton and died at Andover in 1878.
   - Ancestry trees already merge them: one attaches the 1880 Belmont census to our man. Don't take Gilmanton census or deed evidence for our man without checking.
4. **No 1790 head of household.** The 1790 census index shows no Ebenezer Currier heading a household in New Hampshire on the pages I could read. The only Ebenezer is "Ebenr Currier" of Amesbury MA, who is probably the man born 1723. So the 1793 Gilmanton birth is not yet backed by a census.
5. **Richard Currier's baptism (image read).**
   - Record: "Wiltshire, England, Church of England Baptisms, Marriages and Burials, 1538-1812", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/61187/records/588459 : accessed 19 Nov 2026), Salisbury St Thomas, parish registers 1570–1653, image 162 of 296; Wiltshire and Swindon History Centre ref. 1900/5.
   - Under 1616 christenings, March, it reads: "Richard the sonn of Thomas Curryer", 6 March. Before 25 March the year was still 1616, so this is **6 Mar 1616/17**.
   - Evidence that this is our Richard:
     - The court case with his deposition was brought by Capt. Pike, "attorney to the Worshipfull Mr. Francis Dove of Salisbury in old England" (*EQC* 3:147, Salisbury court, April 1664). In it Richard Currier deposed that he "was servant to Mr. Fran. Dove".
     - His ages in 1664 and 1682 match this baptism.
   - **Status: probable.**
   - This also gives Richard's father as **Thomas Curryer**, which supports WikiTree's Currier-32 (Thomas). Nothing here supports Currier-249 (Thomas, born 1553, "Glasgow").
7. **Ebenezer (1793) → Ebenezer (1756): the deed that proves it (image read; FamilySearch signed in).**
   - Record: Merrimack County, NH, Deeds 1825–1826, p. 309. FamilySearch image group 008569610, image 478 of 665 (https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3Q9M-C3QB-1SQG-Y : accessed 19 Nov 2026).
   - Ebenezer Currier Junior of Andover, yeoman, for $170, conveys to "Ebenezer Currier and Stephen Cilley both of said Andover". The deed reads: "meaning hereby to convey **to my father the aforesaid Ebenezer Currier** and said Cilley as tenants in common the premises on which I now live, they being the same premises heretofore conveyed to me by Aaron Cilley".
   - Dated 22 Aug 1825; recorded 26 Aug 1825.
   - Why the people fit:
     - Stephen Cilley was married to Abigail, daughter of Ebenezer and Lydia (Bean) Currier, so he was Junior's brother-in-law.
     - Aaron Cilley was Betsey's grandfather.
     - The father, an Ebenezer Currier of Andover in 1825, is the pensioner, who was 64 in 1820.
   - **The link Ebenezer (1793) → Ebenezer (1756) is now proven.**
8. **Benjamin Cilley's estate, 1765 (abstract read).**
   - Record: *Probate Records of the Province of New Hampshire* (NH State Papers series; the volume covering 1764–1767), "Danville" section, pp. 228–229. FamilySearch Full Text (https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/3:1:3QS7-L9L8-NBY4 : accessed 19 Nov 2026), image 1060 of 1192.
   - What it says:
     - Administration on the estate of "Benjamin Cilley of Hawke [now Danville], gentleman" was granted to **his widow, Judith Cilley**, on 29 Oct 1765.
     - The sureties on the bond were **Job Kenniston of Brentwood** and **John Darling of Kingston**.
     - A deposition records his deathbed words of 22 Sep 1765, at "the Dwelling House of Benjamin Selly of Kingston". He gave his mare and cow to his wife, his oxen to "his two sons John & Moses", and "his Biggest Heifer to his son **Aaron**".
     - The deponents included **Sarah Keniston, wife of Job Keniston**.
   - What it shows:
     - Benjamin (died 1765) was married to **Judith**. A Darling of Kingston stood surety, which fits Judith Darling.
     - **Aaron Cilley (Cilley-177) was his son.**
     - The Kenistons, parents of Apphia, wife of Benjamin born 1742, were at his deathbed and stood surety.
     - The 1742 son Benjamin isn't named. He was married by 1763 and was probably already provided for. So this is **strong circumstantial support**, not proof, that Benjamin (1742) was his son.
   - Note: Betsey's parents, Benjamin (born 1766) and Judith Cilley, daughter of Aaron, were **first cousins**.
9. **Supporting record.** Aaron Cilley died 4 Dec 1862 at Worcester, VT, born about 1799, parents "Benjamin Cilley, Judith" ("Vermont, Vital Records, 1760-1954", FamilySearch, index only). He is Betsey's brother; the Andover history lists an Aaron among her siblings.
10. **Kingston town records.** FamilySearch's indexes show no Kingston birth for Benjamin (1742) and no 1736 marriage. Neither turned up in Full Text either. The Kingston town-book images (unindexed) are the remaining place to look.
6. **Cilley records (before sign-in): not resolved.**
   - Only volume 2 (1718–1740) of the *Probate Records of the Province of New Hampshire* is free online, so I couldn't check estates for Benoni (died before 1746) or Benjamin (died about 1765).
   - Ancestry turns up only family trees for Benjamin Cilley, died 1765, Kingston.
   - The Kingston NH town records (for the 1736 marriage and the 1742 birth) need FamilySearch, which was **signed out** in Chrome this session.
