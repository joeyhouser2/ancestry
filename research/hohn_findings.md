# Anna Hohn / Höhn (WikiTree Hoene-18): parents and origin

Researched 17 November 2026. All records below were viewed on that date. Images were read on screen and nothing was downloaded.

## Short answer

- **Father: Jacob Höhn (Hohn/Hoehn)**, born Nov 1850 in Germany, a grocer in Manhattan in 1900. He was not "Carl". The 1911 marriage certificate (image viewed) names Anna's father as Jacob Hohn. The 1900 census puts Anna in Jacob's household as his daughter.
- **Mother: Margaretha (Margaret) Schmidt**, born 18 Nov 1856 in Germany, died 27 May 1906 in the Bronx. Her father was **Conrad Schmidt**. The marriage certificate gives Anna's mother's maiden name as Margaret Schmidt. Margaret's own death certificate gives her father as Conrad Schmidt. Her gravestone reads "meine geliebte Gattin Margaretha Höhn".
- **Brother: Adam Höhn**, born 30 Nov 1875 in Germany, died 5 Jul 1902 in Manhattan. He is buried with his mother.
- **Stepmother:** her mother died when Anna was about 22. That fits Anna's memory of a stepmother if Jacob remarried after May 1906. No remarriage was found (three NYC Jacob Hohn/Hoehn marriages from 1906–09 were checked and ruled out).
- **"Carl Hoene, 1851–1936" (Hoene-55)** has no source. The WeRelate page has no sources and only gives "b. 1851 Germany, d. 1936". It was evidently built from family memory. The birth year roughly fits Jacob (Nov 1850), but nothing supports the name Carl or the 1936 death. **Recommendation:** correct Hoene-55 to Jacob Höhn (draft below). Joey manages that profile, and Anna is its only child.
- **German home town: still not found.** The records found say only "Germany". The spelling "Höhn" and the name Margaretha suggest southwest Germany (Hesse, Palatinate or Bavaria), but that is a guess.
- **Anna's birth is still open.** The 1900 census says Feb 1883 in Germany, immigrated 1884, and the 1930 census says Germany, arrived 1885. But her 1911 marriage certificate and the 1920 census say she was born in New York City. The family date is 4 Feb 1884. If the 1883 *Rhynland* passenger list is this family (probable), it shows no infant. That would fit a birth in New York in Feb 1884, after the August 1883 arrival. However, no matching NYC birth return was found under Hohn/Hoehn/Hahn, and not every NYC birth of that period was registered.

## Findings with citations

### 1. Marriage certificate, Bronx, 17 Jun 1911: names her parents (key record)
"Certificate and Record of Marriage", no. 1449 (1911), Borough of the Bronx, Alfred Benjamin Bigg Jr. and Anna Hohn; New York City Municipal Archives, Historical Vital Records (https://a860-historicalvitalrecords.nyc.gov/view/8892418 : accessed 17 November 2026), image M-B-1911-0001449.
- Groom: Alfred Benjamin Bigg Jr., 3046 Third Ave, age 23, single, ice cream maker, born New York City. Father Alfred Bigg, mother Hattie Parsons.
- Bride: Anna Hohn, residence 738 St. Ann's Ave (as read), age 27, single, born **New York City**. Father **Jacob Hohn**, mother's maiden name **Margaret Schmidt**. First marriage.
- Married at 338 Alexander Ave, Bronx, by Bertin(?) W. Lee, Rector of St. Mary's Church. Witnesses were William Shovers(?) and a second Shovers.
- On page 2 the bride signed **"Anna Hohn"**. The clerk's handwriting on page 1 explains the misreadings "Hohu" (Ancestry/IGG index) and "Hilser" (FamilySearch).
- **What it proves:** her parents were Jacob Hohn and Margaret Schmidt. This settles the "Carl" question in Jacob's favor. It also shows that the 19 May 1911 Manhattan license (no. 13391) led to the 17 Jun 1911 Bronx ceremony, so FamilySearch's date and place are correct.
- Index entries: "New York, New York, U.S., Extracted Marriage Index, 1866-1937", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/9105/records/2564334 : accessed 17 November 2026), Anna Hohu and Alfred BJr Bigg, 17 Jun 1911, Bronx, cert. 1449.
- Church entry: "New York, U.S., Episcopal Diocese of New York Church Records, 1767-1970", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/61224/records/900254562 : accessed 17 November 2026), Anna Heilm and Alfred Benjamin Bigg, marriage 17 Jun 1911, St Mary's. This is St. Mary's Episcopal Church, Mott Haven, Bronx. The register image was not viewed.

### 2. 1900 census, Manhattan: Anna in Jacob and Margaret's household
"1900 United States Federal Census", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/7602/records/66919373 : accessed 17 November 2026), Jacob Hohn household, 833 First Avenue, Manhattan, New York, New York; ED 0589, sheet 7, lines 93–96; NARA T623, roll 1107. Daughter's entry: https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/7602/records/66919376 (indexed as "Horn Hohn").
- Jacob Hohn, head, b. Nov 1850 Germany, 49, married 24 years (about 1876), immigrated 1884, naturalized, grocer.
- Margaret, wife, b. Nov 1856 Germany, 43, mother of 2 children, 2 living.
- Adam, son, b. Nov 1875 Germany, 20, grocer.
- **Annie**, daughter, b. **Feb 1883 Germany**, 17, single, immigrated 1884.
- Also in the household: Felix Powell, boarder.
- On the image the enumerator swapped the name and relationship columns for line 96. The name column has "Dau'r" (indexed as "Horn") and the relationship column has "Annie". I checked this on the image.
- **What it proves:** Anna was the daughter of Jacob and Margaret. Margaret was then the mother of only 2 living children, Adam and Anna, so Margaret was Anna's birth mother, not a stepmother. The census birth month (February) agrees with the family's 4 February.

### 3. Margaret's death, Bronx, 27 May 1906: gives her father's name
"Certificate and Record of Death", no. 2762 (1906), Borough of the Bronx, Margaret Hoehn; NYC Municipal Archives (https://a860-historicalvitalrecords.nyc.gov/view/4125958 : accessed 17 November 2026), image D-B-1906-0002762.
- Age 49 years 6 months 9 days. That works out to 18 Nov 1856, which matches the gravestone. She was married, a housewife, born Germany, 21 years in the US and in NYC.
- She died at 629 E. 154th St (private house) of chronic valvular heart disease and nephritis.
- Father **Conrad Schmidt**, born Germany. The mother's name is blank and her birthplace is given as Germany.
- She was buried in "Lutheran" cemetery on 29 May 1906. The undertaker was Louis B. ?itzenberger of 667 Melrose Ave.
- Index: "New York, New York, U.S., Extracted Death Index, 1862-1948", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/9131/records/1077057 : accessed 17 November 2026).
- **What it proves:** Margaret's maiden name was Schmidt (her father was Conrad Schmidt). The family was in the Bronx by 1906, a few blocks from Anna's 1911 address.

### 4. Gravestone of Margaretha and Adam Höhn, All Faiths (formerly Lutheran) Cemetery
Find a Grave, memorial 133020303, Margaretha Hohn (https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/133020303/margaretha-hohn : accessed 17 November 2026), with gravestone photo by Lisa F. (2014). Linked son: memorial 133020376, Adam Hohn (https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/133020376/adam-hohn).
- Inscription, in German: "Ruht in Frieden – meine geliebte Gattin Margaretha Höhn, geb. 18 Nov. 1856, gest. 27 Mai 1906 – und Sohn Adam Höhn, geb. 30 Nov. 1875, gest. 5 Jul 1902".
- **What it proves:** the family spelled the name **Höhn**. The stone was raised by her husband ("meine geliebte Gattin"). It gives exact dates for Margaretha and Adam.
- Adam's death index: Ancestry, NYC Extracted Death Index (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/9131/records/1557712 : accessed 17 November 2026), Adam Hohn, 25, 5 Jul 1902, Manhattan, cert. 20097. This certificate is not digitized on the NYC site.

### 5. Probable arrival: *Rhynland*, Antwerp to New York, 10 Aug 1883
"New York, U.S., Arriving Passenger and Crew Lists, 1820-1957", Ancestry: Jacob Hohn, 32 (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/7488/records/11769566), Mary Hohn, 26 (…/records/11769567), Adam Hohn, 8 (…/records/11769568), all accessed 17 November 2026; NARA M237, roll 469, image NYM237_469-0127.
- On the image the three appear together. Jacob is 32 (his occupation may read "Wirth", innkeeper), Mary is 26 and Adam is 8. Each is listed from Germany. **No infant is listed.**
- **What it suggests:** the ages match Jacob (b. Nov 1850), Margaretha (b. Nov 1856) and Adam (b. Nov 1875) exactly. The censuses say 1884 and Margaret's death certificate implies about 1885, so this is probable but not proven. If this is the family, Anna was either born after arrival (New York, about Feb 1884, as the 1911 and 1920 records say) or was left off the list. The list gives no town of origin.
- A second Jacob Hohn, 32, arrived on the *Schiedam* from Rotterdam on 10 Dec 1883 (…/records/12662346), alone. It was not examined further.

### 6. 1920 census: Anna born New York
"1920 United States Federal Census", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/6061/records/18782046 : accessed 17 November 2026), Alfred Bigg household, Ave E, Bayonne Ward 3, Hudson, New Jersey.
- Anna is 35, born **New York**, with both parents born in Germany. Alfred is 32, son Alfred 6, and boarder Alfred Moore 34.
- **What it shows:** a second record, besides the marriage certificate, giving a New York birth. It conflicts with the 1900 and 1930 censuses, which say Germany.

### 7. Possible death of Jacob: Manhattan, 2 Nov 1932 (not proven)
"Certificate of Death", no. 25248 (1932), Manhattan, Jacob Hoahn; NYC Municipal Archives (https://a860-historicalvitalrecords.nyc.gov/view/6474364 : accessed 17 November 2026).
- He was a widower, age 83 (born about 1849), a street cleaner, born Germany, said to have lived 35 years in the US. He died at Bellevue Hospital of arteriosclerosis and a fractured right femur. The medical examiner handled the case.
- His usual residence was **461 Brook Avenue, Bronx**, in the same Mott Haven neighborhood as the family's 1906 and 1911 addresses. His parents' names are blank, and the burial place is "City" (the public burial ground).
- **Assessment:** possibly Anna's father. The name, age and birthplace fit, and so does the neighborhood. But it has no informant, no names of relatives, and the wrong "years in US", so it is not proven. The family's death year of 1936 does not match, but that year is from memory.

### 7a. Probable naturalization: Superior Court of the City of New York, 1886–1888
"New York, U.S., State and Federal Naturalization Records, 1794-1943", Ancestry: declaration of intention (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/2280/records/336039 : accessed 17 November 2026) and oath of allegiance (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/2280/records/336040); Superior Court of the City of New York, bundle 432, record 2; citing National Archives at New York City, Petitions for Naturalization, 1793-1906, NAI 5324244, RG 85.
- **Declaration**, 4 Jun 1886: "Jacob Höhn" renounces allegiance to the "Emperor of Germany". This is the standard printed wording for any subject of the German Empire, so it says nothing about his home town.
- **Oath**, 24 Oct 1888: Jacob Höhn, occupation **brewer**, residence 167(?) First Ave. His witness was George Rockelein(?) of 2?4 11th Ave, who swore that he had known Jacob in the US for 5 years.
- **Assessment:** probably our Jacob. The spelling Höhn, the First Avenue address, the 5-year wait after an 1883 arrival, and "naturalized" in the 1900 census all fit. However, his occupation is "brewer", not the "grocer" of 1900. **No German town is given.** The witness may be worth tracing; he could be a relative or someone from the same village.

### 7b. Searches with no useful result (17 Nov 2026)
- **German Genealogy Group naturalization index** (germangenealogygroup.com): Hohn/Hoehn/Hoene entries found are all different people (Bronx 1914+, Brooklyn federal court, NJ 1935). The index doesn't cover Manhattan state courts before 1906.
- **Ancestry U.S. City Directories (collection 2469)**, NYC, Jacob Hohn/Hoehn, 1899–1915: every hit is a different man (a carpenter at 653 Courtlandt Ave in 1899, a polisher on Diamond St in 1904, Brooklyn engineers and bartenders, a poultry dealer in 1915). Ancestry's coverage of Manhattan directories for 1884–1898 is thin.
- **1905 NY state census (Ancestry 7364)**: the only Jacob Hohn in the Bronx was 42 with wife Caroline (a different family). No Margaret or Anna Hohn/Hoehn of the right age in Manhattan or the Bronx.
- **1910 federal census and 1915 NY state census**: no match for Jacob (b. about 1850) or Anna (b. about 1883–84). The family is probably mis-indexed, as Anna was in 1900.
- **NYS Historic Newspapers**: no hits for Jacob, Margaretha or Adam Hoehn/Hohn. The site covers mostly upstate papers.
- **Chronicling America**: the Library of Congress site blocked automated searching, so it was not searched. It needs to be searched by hand.

### 7c. German-origin search, 18 Nov 2026 (FamilySearch signed in)
- **Spellings.** Donald's letters spell the name **Hoene** (2022) and **Hoehn** (2026); records also use Hohn, Höhn, Hoahn and Hohu. These are all one surname: "oe" is the standard way to type "ö", and Hoene is a Hoehn variant. All the searches below used the spellings Höhn, Hoehn, Hohn and Hoene, plus Ancestry and FamilySearch's sound-alike matching, so the spelling is not what is blocking the search.
- **FamilySearch German indexes:**
  - Margaretha Schmidt, b. 1856, father Conrad: none matching.
  - Adam Höhn/Hoehn, b. 1875, father Jacob: none matching. There are near misses: an Adam Hohn b. 30 Apr 1875 in the Saar (father Daniel), an Adam Höhn b. 12 May 1875 at Wilhelmsfeld, Baden (father Martin), and an Adam Horn b. Jun 1875 in Michelstadt, Hesse. All have the wrong date or the wrong father.
  - A marriage of Jacob Höhn and Margaretha Schmidt: none.
  - **Conclusion:** the family's home parish has probably not been indexed online yet. Many German parishes are filmed but not indexed.
- **Ruled out: Hesse civil marriage, Frankfurt am Main, 17 Mar 1877** ("Hesse, Germany, Marriages, 1849-1930", Ancestry 61118/2698316). This Jacob Höhn was born **21 Nov 1850**, the same month as our Jacob, and his parents were Conrad and Elisabetha. But he married **Katharina Wilhelmine Eleonore Motzer** in 1877, while our Jacob had been married to Margaretha since about 1876 and had a son born in 1875. A different man, unless Jacob married twice; nothing suggests he did.
- **Naturalization witness George Rockelein (Röckelein):**
  - In 1900 Ann Rockelein (40, born Germany) headed a household at 207 W. 66th St, Manhattan, with five children. George Sr. was evidently dead by then.
  - A George Rockelein, born about 1847 in **Bamberg** (Upper Franconia, Bavaria), appears in New York Civil War muster rolls (Ancestry 1965/624242). He is probably the witness.
  - Röckelein is a typically Franconian surname. This is only a weak regional hint: a witness was often just a neighbor or employer.
- **Ancestry "U.S., Evangelical Lutheran Church in America Church Records" (60722):** no New York entries for Höhn/Hohn. The family's NYC congregation isn't in that collection.

## Still open / next steps
1. **German home town.** The best sources are:
   - Jacob's naturalization. He was "naturalized" by 1900, probably in a New York County court between about 1888 and 1900. Petitions from that period rarely give the town.
   - St. Mary's Episcopal register image (Ancestry 61224/900254562). It might give the bride's birthplace.
   - Adam's 1902 death certificate (Manhattan 20097, which must be ordered) and his burial record at All Faiths/Lutheran Cemetery.
   - A German Lutheran church marriage or baptism in Manhattan, 1883–85, if Anna was baptized in NYC.
   - The Hesse marriage index has a Jacob Höhn married 17 Mar 1877 in Frankfurt am Main (Ancestry 61118/2698316). It was not checked, and the year is one too late for "married 1876".
2. **Anna's birth.** Possible sources: an NYC birth return around Feb 1884 under another spelling, or her Social Security application (SS-5), which would give her birthplace and parents. She was in Bayonne in 1936 or later, so an SS-5 may exist. The SSACI search under "Anna Bigg" did not find her.
3. **The stepmother.** Look for Jacob's remarriage after May 1906, in the Bronx or NJ, under Höhn/Hoehn/Hohn/Hahn. Also try to find the 1910 census of Jacob and Anna, probably in the Bronx near St. Ann's Ave. Neither was found in Ancestry's index.
4. **Jacob's death.** Confirm or reject the 1932 Jacob Hoahn, and search for a 1936 death in the Bronx and in Hudson County, NJ.
