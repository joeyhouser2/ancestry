# Military service: mother's-side direct ancestors (WWI and earlier)

Researched 18 November 2026 on Ancestry (signed in, via Chrome), the WikiTree API and the TNA Discovery API.
Scope: Joey's **mother's** direct-line men born roughly 1723–1900. WWII and later are left out, and so are
siblings, cousins and in-laws. Record links are Ancestry record pages unless noted. All were accessed 18 Nov 2026.

## Summary

Four direct ancestors on the mother's side have documented military service. Two of them were Revolutionary War
soldiers on the Currier/Cilley line, which reaches Joey through Lillian Currier (Currier-800), the mother of Joey's mother's mother. **Ebenezer Currier (1756–1840, Currier-1180)**
was a New Hampshire Continental private in Col. Pierse Long's regiment, Aug 1776–Aug 1777. His own 1818 pension
declaration was made at Andover NH; his payments were later sent to Norwich VT, where he died; and his widow Mercy
(Quimby) also received a pension. These facts tie the file to him securely. **Benjamin Cilley (1742–1823, Cilley-175)**
of Andover NH is a recognised SAR patriot who served in 1775 in Capt. Jacob Webster's company. There are also two
Civil War Union soldiers. **Martin Murphy (c.1831/33–1911)** of Weymouth MA, Hannah Murphy's father, served
three years in Co. B, 9th Massachusetts Infantry (1861–64): the soldier's residence, his Irish birth and his death on
25 Jun 1911 all match. **William E. Avery (c.1842–1891)**, Hattie Avery's father, is very probably the William E. Avery
of New York City who served in Co. A, 17th NY Infantry (1861–63) and Co. G, 6th NY Heavy Artillery (to 1865). His widow
Lizzie filed for a pension on 25 Sep 1891, a month after the probable Manhattan death of 26 Aug 1891. Five WWI draft
registrations were found (Alfred B. Bigg Jr, John McGlynn, James Thomas McElligott and a probable Joseph F. O'Donnell),
but none of these men is known to have served. Nothing was found for the English (Sheppey, Milton, Lincolnshire) men
in TNA's WO 97, WO 121, ADM 139, ADM 29 or ADM 73 indexes. The Irish, German and Hungarian immigrants were outside
any record set covered here. Amendment drafts for the four veterans are in `research/veterans_drafts/`.

## Detail and citations for the veterans

### Ebenezer Currier (1756–1840), Currier-1180: Revolutionary War

- **Declaration, 23 April 1818:** "Ebenezer Currier of Andover in the County of Hillsborough and State of New Hampshire" enlisted on the Continental establishment in **August 1776 for one year**. He served in **Capt. Timothy Clement's company, Col. Pierse Long's regiment, New Hampshire line**, and was discharged in **August 1777 at Stillwater**. A fellow soldier, Samuel Martin of Salisbury, confirmed his service. Pensioned at $8 a month from 23 Apr 1818 (the Act of 18 March 1818) as "Ebenezer Currier 2d". Payments were transferred in March 1831 to **Norwich, Windsor County, Vermont**.
- **Widow:** Mercy Currier, "widow of Ebenezer Currier 2d", pensioned from 3 Feb 1853 at $96.50 a year. She died 21 Nov 1855. The file holds a certified Springfield NH town record: "Mr Ebenezer Currier of Andover with Mrs Mercy Quimby of Springfield were joined in marriage November fourteenth 1816". Bounty land warrant 14751-160-55.
- **Why this is our man:** WikiTree's Currier-1180 lived at Andover NH (his son Ebenezer, Currier-1179, died there) and died at Norwich, Windsor County, VT in 1840. His first wife Lydia Bean died in 1814, two years before the Quimby marriage.
- **Not ours:** the second NH file, Ebenezer Currier S.45715 (NH, 30 pp.), belongs to another Ebenezer, the one this man was "2d" to. The 1777–1783 1st NH Battalion rolls (Ancestry 4282 / 1309) probably belong to him too, since our man was discharged in 1777.
- Citation: "U.S., Revolutionary War Pension and Bounty-Land Warrant Application Files, 1800-1900", database with images, Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/1995/records/14945 : accessed 18 November 2026), Ebenezer Currier, W. 724 (widow Mercy), Vermont, 54 pp.; images 47–56 viewed (declaration 23 Apr 1818; pension cards; 1816 marriage certificate); citing NARA M804, roll 715.
- Supporting: "New Hampshire, U.S., Revolutionary War Records, 1675-1835", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/61902/records/21684 : accessed 18 November 2026), Ebenezer Currier, private, 14 Jan 1777, muster rolls. This roll was not examined, and it could belong to either Ebenezer.

### Martin Murphy (c.1831/33–1911), Weymouth MA: Civil War, Union

- Private, **Co. B, 9th Massachusetts Infantry** (the Irish regiment). Enlisted 11 Jun 1861, aged 28, born about 1833 in **Ireland**, a **shoemaker of Weymouth Landing**. Mustered out at Boston on 21 Jun 1864. GAR member. **Died 25 Jun 1911.**
- 1890 veterans schedule: Martin Murphy, Weymouth, Norfolk Co. MA (ED 686), private, Co. B, 9th Mass. Inf., 11 Jun 1861–11 Jun 1864.
- **Why this is our man:** Hannah L. Murphy (born c.1860 **Weymouth**) was the daughter of Martin Murphy and Bridget Collins (her 1925 death record), and she was buried at Weymouth. FamilySearch's shared tree gives Martin's dates as 1831–**1911**. The Weymouth residence, the 1911 death and the Irish birth all agree. Remaining gap: his death record naming Bridget, to remove any doubt.
- Citations: "U.S., Civil War Soldier Records and Profiles, 1861-1865", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/1555/records/2139248 : accessed 18 November 2026), Martin Murphy, Co. B, 9th Mass. Inf., citing *Massachusetts Soldiers, Sailors and Marines in the Civil War* and GAR Dept. of Massachusetts records. "1890 Veterans Schedules of the U.S. Federal Census", Ancestry (https://www.ancestry.com/search/collections/8667/records/61579 : accessed 18 November 2026), Martin Murphy, Weymouth, ED 686, citing NARA M123.
- Not found: a Civil War pension index card definitely his (260 Martin Murphys; none tied to the 9th Mass. in the first pages). Worth a second look.

### William E. Avery (c.1842–1891), Hattie's father: Civil War, Union (probable)

- **6th NY Heavy Artillery, Co. G:** William E. Avery, private (NPS Soldiers & Sailors, M551 roll 4).
- **1890 veterans schedule:** William E. Avery, **New York City** (ED 417), private, Co. A, 17th NY Inf., 19 Apr 1861 – 26 Jul 1865.
- **Pension index card:** William E. Avery, A 17 NY Inf. / G 6 NY H.A.; **invalid** application 287,279, filed 21 May 1879; **widow Lizzie Avery**, application 527,367, filed **25 Sep 1891**, New York. No certificate numbers are shown.
- **Why probably ours:** Hattie's father was William Avery, born New York, and FamilySearch's tree (a clue) gives him as William **Edward** Avery, 1843–1891. Emily had left him and remarried by 1880. OPEN_LEADS #10 gives a possible death on **26 Aug 1891 in Manhattan** (cert. 30213) and a possible 1881 remarriage. A widow "Lizzie" applying a month after that death fits both. The 17th NY Infantry was raised in NYC and Westchester (Yonkers), which fits a Manhattan family. **To prove it:** order the pension file (NARA, invalid application 287279 / widow's application 527367). A widow's file normally names his earlier marriage and children.
